The Buyers' Club is a project of the North Oakland Assembly, a collective of neighbors working on mutual aid, ICE resistance, and more. @karo, one of the longtime organizers, described how organizing with immigrant farmers in Watsonville brings resilience to all of us.

**What is a "buyers' club"? How did this come about?**

"The project was seeded during conversations that took place in the Land Reform Winter School in early 2026 at the Gill Tract Community Farm. The idea is modeled on tactics of peasant and indigenous led land reform movements in Bolivia and Brazil (the MST): farmers organize amongst themselves to collectivize supply, while urban consumers organize amongst themselves to aggregate demand, pool money, and handle distribution logistics. The result is more money to the farmers (by cutting out distributors), cheaper food for consumers (also by cutting out distributors), and real, material mechanisms for building solidarity between rural-urban communities."

**How do you build relationships with the farmers?**

"Every month, 'solidarity brigades' are organized to bring buyers out to the farms, meet the farmers, and work the land with them. At the last brigade, we weeded strawberry fields and prepared an area to be planted with flowers.

One thing that came out of the recent brigade is that folks from the Bay Area will likely assist farmers in preparing for El Niño. Many of the farms are in a major floodplain and the last El Niño in 2016 was a major crisis for them."

**What are you all working towards?**

Politically, we want to build towards collective visioning together: directly provisioning food-centered mutual aid in the Bay Area, changing the landscape of our food system overall by divesting from corporate distributors, participating in the struggle against Big Ag poisoning our land and water, and intervening in the state's control over our land and water.
